Privacy

The app stores its data on the phone and never goes online, so it sends nothing anywhere itself. The user-facing privacy policy says so in plain words and records the answers given in Google Play's data safety form; this page is the contract that keeps the policy true.

Invariant — The policy states only what the app does. A pull request that changes what can leave the device (network access, analytics, crash reporting, any SDK that sends data) or the declared permission set must update the privacy policy, including its "Play data safety form" answers, in the same pull request.

  • PRIV-001 The app declares no INTERNET permission. Nothing the app stores can be sent anywhere by the app itself.
  • PRIV-002 App data is stored only on the device: the Room database (stretches, schedules and reminder times, daily goals and their history, logged stretches with their time, skipped dates) and the scheduler's local preferences (the trigger time of each armed alarm and of the last notified occurrence, SCHED-005, NOTIF-007). There are no accounts, analytics, ads, crash reporting or third-party SDKs that collect data.
  • PRIV-003 The declared permission set is exactly:
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS: show stretch reminders (NOTIF-005);
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED: re-arm reminders after a restart;
  • SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM: fire reminders on time (SCHED-007).

Adding or removing a permission is a change to this requirement and to the policy. - PRIV-004 The privacy policy page covers PRIV-001 to PRIV-003 in plain words, and also: that Android's device backup may copy all of the app's data to the user's Google account, and device-to-device transfer may copy it to a new phone, both handled by Android and Google rather than the developer; how to delete the data (clear the app's storage, or uninstall); that the app is not directed at children under 13; how the policy changes and how to get in touch; and the answers recorded for Play's data safety form. Because the backup wording says "all of the app's data", the manifest keeps android:allowBackup="true" on <application> with no android:fullBackupContent and no android:dataExtractionRules; ManifestPermissionsTest checks this.

Build checklist

  • [x] ManifestPermissionsTest reads the source manifest and asserts no INTERNET (PRIV-001), exactly the three permissions above (PRIV-003), and allowBackup="true" with no backup rules (PRIV-004).
  • [x] The policy page lists what is stored, each permission, backup, deletion, children, changes and contact, and the data safety answers (PRIV-002, PRIV-004).
  • [ ] The merged manifest is not checked: a library could in theory merge in a permission.
  • [x] Store listing reviewed: this page is store: omit, and any claim in docs/store/listing.md that cites it is still true (STORE-LIST-002, STORE-LIST-003).
