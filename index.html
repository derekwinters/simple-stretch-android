Simple Stretch

A small Android app that reminds you to stretch during the day, on your own schedule.

Features

  • Stretch library ("My stretches" in the home screen's menu): add, edit and delete stretches (name, instructions, optional duration). Ten common stretches are added on first run. You can also create a stretch straight from the goals screen and the reminder-time picker with "New stretch…".
  • Schedules: as many as you like, each with a name, an on/off switch and the days of the week it runs. A schedule either has set times, each optionally with its own stretches (e.g. neck rolls at 10:00, hip flexors at 14:00), or repeats: every N minutes at a minute past the hour between two times, e.g. every hour at :50 from 8:00 to 17:00 (8:50 ... 16:50; the end time is inclusive).
  • Daily goals: e.g. 3 hamstring stretches and 3 calf stretches a day, set with a − / + stepper next to every stretch on the goals screen (0 means no goal). The home screen shows today's progress ("Hamstring stretch 2/3"). Once every goal for the day is met, reminders stay quiet for the rest of the day.
  • Trends ("Trends" in the home screen's menu, or on the goals card): a week or month bar chart of each day's score (goal stretches done, up to each target, out of the day's total target), the period's average, how many days met every goal, and your current streak. Tap a day to see its goals, the score climbing through the day, and every stretch logged with its time. Skipped days and days without goals are gaps, not 0%; changing a goal never rewrites past days.
  • Home screen widget ("Today's goals", 2×2, resizable): a ring with one arc per daily goal, each filling as you do that stretch, around the app's stretch figure and today's count (a check mark once every goal is met). It follows light/dark mode and your wallpaper colours, starts again at 0 at midnight, and opens the app when tapped.
  • Stretch sessions: from a reminder or the home screen's Stretch now, tick the stretches you did and save. Opened from a reminder, that reminder's stretches lead in their own section; otherwise stretches still short of today's goal do. Everything else follows alphabetically, and nothing is pre-checked.
  • Skip a day: one tap on "Skip today", at the right of the home screen's "Today" heading (or in the top bar's menu), silences every reminder for the rest of the day; "Resume today" undoes it. Future days can be skipped from the calendar screen, and skipped days can be removed again.
  • Notifications show the stretches (or today's unmet goals), with Start (opens the stretch session) and Snooze 5 min buttons; swipe to dismiss just this one. Tapping also opens the session.
  • Reminders survive reboots and time or time-zone changes, and use exact alarms when Android allows them (falling back to slightly-less-punctual alarms when it doesn't).

Behaviour is set out in the specification.

Requirements

  • Android 8.0 (API 26) or later.
  • To build: JDK 17+, Android SDK with platform 36 and build-tools 36.0.0 (set ANDROID_HOME or sdk.dir in local.properties).

Build and install

./gradlew test             # unit tests
./gradlew assembleDebug    # app/build/outputs/apk/debug/simple-stretch-<version>-debug.apk
adb install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/simple-stretch-*-debug.apk

Or copy the APK to the phone and open it (allow installs from that source when asked).

Application ID: from 0.3.0 the application ID is io.github.derekwinters.simple_stretch_android. An install of 0.1.0 or 0.2.0 (com.derekwinters.stretch) is a separate app: it will not update to new builds and keeps its own data until uninstalled. Nothing is carried over, so the new app starts empty, and the old one can be uninstalled whenever you like.

Every pull request's CI run also uploads its debug APK as the simple-stretch-debug-apk workflow artifact (kept for 14 days). Pull requests from branches of this repository also upload simple-stretch-release-apk, signed with the release key, which installs in place over a release of 0.3.0 or later, so a PR can be tried before it is released without losing data. A debug APK and a release-signed APK cannot be installed over each other. Those pull requests also upload simple-stretch-release-aab, the release Android App Bundle signed with the same key; the AAB is the upload format for Google Play and cannot be installed directly, so sideload the APK.

On first launch, allow notifications. On Android 12/12L, also allow "Alarms & reminders" if the app shows the banner, so reminders arrive on the minute.

Store listing

The Google Play screenshots are rendered from the app's screens on the JVM, not captured on a phone (store listing specification), and committed under fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/; their captions are in store/captions.md. The store icon (images/icon.png, drawn from the app's launcher icon) and the feature graphic (images/featureGraphic.png: a headline, whose text is also in captions.md, beside the Home and stretch session screens on two tilted phones) are rendered and checked the same way, by the same commands.

./gradlew verifyRoborazziDebug   # compare with the committed store images (what CI runs)
./gradlew recordRoborazziDebug   # re-record them, deliberately, after changing a screen, caption or the launcher icon

Every pull request's CI run verifies them; a changed or missing image fails the run and uploads the new and diff images as the store-screenshot-diffs workflow artifact. That run never records them. To re-record, either:

  • apply the record-store-screenshots label to the pull request. CI records the images and pushes them as one commit to the branch store-screenshots/pr-<number> (never to the pull request's branch), says so in its job summary and removes the label. Bring that commit in with your own push (git fetch origin store-screenshots/pr-<number> && git merge --ff-only FETCH_HEAD), so the pull request's CI run verifies it; or
  • run ./gradlew recordRoborazziDebug locally, on Linux with JDK 17 as CI runs, and commit the images.

Either way the new images are reviewed in the pull request's diff.

The listing's text (title, short description and full description) is written by hand in store/listing.md, one claim per line, each citing the requirement IDs it rests on. After editing it, compile it into fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/ and commit the result:

python3 .github/scripts/store_listing.py           # write title.txt, short_description.txt, full_description.txt
python3 .github/scripts/store_listing.py --check   # what CI runs; writes nothing

Every pull request's CI run fails when a claim cites a missing or retired requirement, a headline feature page (marked <!-- store: headline -->) has no claim, a text is over Google Play's limit, or the committed files differ from the listing.

Releases

Merging to main runs release-please, which keeps a release PR up to date from Conventional Commit titles. Merging that PR tags a release and attaches three files:

  • simple-stretch-<version>-release.apk, for installing directly onto a phone (sideloading);
  • simple-stretch-<version>-release.aab, the Android App Bundle for uploading to Google Play;
  • store-listing-<version>.zip, the reviewed Google Play listing (text, screenshots, store icon and feature graphic) as committed at the release, under fastlane/metadata/android/. The release only checks and packages it; it never writes listing text.

Both are signed with a stable release key (ADR 0002) and checked by the same signature gate before they are attached, so each release from 0.3.0 on upgrades the installed app in place and keeps its data. The same key is Google Play's app signing key (ADR 0004), so an install from Google Play and a sideloaded release APK share one signature and can update each other in place to the same or a newer version.

Each release's version code is derived from its version (0.3.0 is 300; see ADR 0003), so it rises with every release, as Google Play and Android require. Before a release is attached, a guard checks that its code is higher than the previous release's. The same check runs on the open release PR after every merge to main: a release PR whose code is not higher than the last release's gets a failing release-version-code status.

One-time step: builds made before release signing (debug-signed releases, CI or local debug APKs) use a different key. Uninstall those once before installing the first release-signed build; after that, upgrades are in place. The same applies to moving from the old application ID: 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 (com.derekwinters.stretch) do not upgrade to 0.3.0 or later, which install alongside them as a new, empty app; uninstall the old one when you no longer need it.

Project layout

app/src/main/java/com/derekwinters/stretch/
  data/           Room entities, DAOs, database (+ seed, migrations), repository
  goals/          goal progress, goal history and session ordering maths (pure Kotlin)
  trends/         daily score, periods, streak and intraday series maths (pure Kotlin)
  scheduling/     next-occurrence and repeating-slot maths, AlarmManager scheduling, snooze,
                  alarm + reschedule receivers
  notifications/  notification channel/content and the Snooze / Skip actions
  widget/         segmented ring home screen widget: ring layout and palette (pure Kotlin),
                  bitmap renderer, provider and redraws
  ui/             Compose screens: home, schedule editor, stretch session, goals, trends
                  (Canvas charts) and day detail, stretch library, skipped days
app/src/test/     JVM unit tests (next occurrence, repeating slots, goal maths, goal history,
                  trends, widget ring and palette) and the store image tests (store/)
app/schemas/      exported Room schemas
docs/spec/        requirements with IDs; docs/adr/ decisions; docs/store/ listing text and
                  screenshot captions
fastlane/         Google Play listing text (compiled from docs/store/listing.md) and images

Notes

  • Exact reminders need the user-granted SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM ("Alarms & reminders") on Android 12 and later. On Android 14 and later it starts denied on a new install, so reminders are inexact (may arrive a few minutes late) until the user taps Allow on the home-screen banner. USE_EXACT_ALARM is deliberately not declared: Google Play restricts it to alarm, timer and calendar apps.
  • Some manufacturers aggressively stop background apps; if reminders go missing, exempt the app from battery optimisation.
